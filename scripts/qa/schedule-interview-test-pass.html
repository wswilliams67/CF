This walks the Schedule Interview form the way an operator meets it — date, duration and the availability planner, then interview type, then attendees, then the message — and out through validation, cancel and success.

Every expected value below was read off the Figma board Schedule Interview | Dark, not from memory, so a wrong-but-plausible result gets caught rather than waved through. Three checks guard values the design only settled on 09-15-2026 — they name the old value as well as the right one, because a build started earlier may still carry it.

A

Getting in

The case hands its name to the form. If the wrong case arrives, nothing after this section means anything.

B

Interview Date

The picker is permanently on screen — it is not a dropdown. Everything in section C recalculates from whatever date is selected here.

C

Duration and soonest availability

Two radios drive an Outlook scan. The result is three slots every attendee has free in common — the "Top 3".

D

The availability planner

A panel that slides in beside the attendee list showing every attendee's calendar as coloured blocks. The heaviest part of the screen and the most specified.

E

Interview type

Three mutually exclusive buttons, each swapping the field beneath them for a different one.

F

Inviting attendees

Three separate invite fields — the interviewee, internal staff, and outside email addresses — each with its own search and its own Invite button.

G

Required attendance and removal

One card, two controls: the body toggles required attendance, the button removes the person. They must not be reachable by the same click.

H

Subject and message

The last two fields, and the only rich text on the screen.

I

Validation, cancel and success

The primary button is the form's only completeness indicator, so it has to be right.

J

The action bar

The form is taller than the viewport at every realistic size, so the bar has a specification of its own.
