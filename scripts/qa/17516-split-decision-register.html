Only three of these are product decisions. Four are questions about what the ledger and the projection builder can already do — they need routing to engineering, not deciding. Yours to decide

Product and policy calls. Nothing external answers these.

S-1 Blocking B · B2 · C · D

Can a legal hold be inherited by the person a split creates?

The screens currently assert that it can. B, B3, B4 and B5 show On Legal Hold · 2 matters on both people, and B1, B2, C and D show Acme v. Byrne — inherited on the new person. The per-badge “Inherited” label was dropped on 08-19, so the claim is now carried by the matching counts rather than by a word — which makes it easy to ship without anyone noticing it was never ruled on.

The split concept (§6) asserts inheritance but defers to merge §2, which is itself blocking and owned by whoever owns spoliation risk. Merge §2 offers three options:

  • ARewrite the hold’s custodian list.
  • BLeave the hold where it is and give the identity a redirect.
  • CRefuse the operation entirely while a hold is in force.

Why it blocks: C removes four screens rather than restyling them. And the workflow-attribution panel (B3/B4) deliberately does not offer legal holds as an attributable workflow — that choice was made pending this ruling and has to be revisited either way.

Needs legal in the room, not just product. It is a spoliation-risk question wearing a UI costume.

S-2 Open B3 · B4

Must every case be attributed before the split can be applied?

The attribution panel lets workflows be assigned for all cases at once or case by case. What it does not say is whether APPLY ATTRIBUTIONS is available before every case has been decided.

  • AA default exists — unattributed workflows stay with the original person — so APPLY is always live and the panel is optional. This is what the screens currently imply.
  • BAttribution is required, so APPLY stays disabled until every case is set — matching the house rule that a primary CTA is disabled until the form validates.

The trade: B is safer for a split that divides preservation-relevant work, but it makes the panel mandatory on a flow where most splits are clean.

Bundled with it: the panel has no empty state. A person on no cases has nothing to attribute, so CHOOSE WORKFLOW ATTRIBUTIONS should probably not be offered at all in that case — worth confirming while you are here.

S-3 Open Edge §7

In shadow mode, is Split hidden or shown-disabled?

With no projection authority the action cannot run. Hiding it leaves no explanation for why a control someone expects is missing; disabling it needs copy stating why.

Currently drawn as shown-disabled with an explanatory state, which is the more informative of the two and consistent with how the rest of this feature set treats unavailable actions. Confirm or overturn.

Questions for engineering

Not decisions — facts about what already exists. Each one changes what the interface is allowed to promise, so the answer shapes the build either way.

S-4 High B

Is a dry-run rebuild available?

The entire preview assumes we can retract hypothetically and get the resulting projection back without committing. #17378 verifies a real retraction and rebuild; no hypothetical path is called out anywhere.

Without one the interface can only offer a button whose effect it cannot describe — on the operation that exists to correct irreversible mistakes.

Fallback if the answer is no: commit-then-undo, which inverts the preview-before-consequence pattern used everywhere else in this feature set. Workable, but it is a different design, not a smaller one.

⏱ Timing — retrofitting a dry-run onto a projection builder written only to commit is materially harder than building it in. This needs answering while #17377 is in flight.

S-5 High Foundation · C

Are assertions individually retractable — and is a retraction itself an appended assertion?

The whole model rests on this. “Recorded, not erased” on screen C, and the history claim on both resulting people, depend on the ledger appending a retraction rather than deleting a row.

If the ledger deletes instead: screen C’s central promise is false and the audit story for a split has to be rewritten before anything is built.

S-6 High A

Is an operator-asserted join supported, and is it distinguishable from a key-derived one?

Move to a specific person is a retraction plus a manual join. Two parts: can the ledger record that at all, and can a later reviewer tell a human decision from a machine match?

Why the second half matters as much as the first: if the two are indistinguishable in the record, every operator-moved identity looks like the matcher’s work when the log is read back — which is exactly the question a declaration would be asked.

S-7 Open D

Is the rebuild synchronous per person, or queued?

Determines whether the After state shows a progress indicator at all.

Why it is not cosmetic: pfizerpoc holds 61,888 persons, so a rebuild may not be instant. A person page showing a stale projection with no indication is worse than one that says plainly it is rebuilding.

Falls out of the answers above

Do not put this one to anyone directly — it resolves itself once S-5 and S-6 are answered.

S-8 High B2

Can an identity re-home onto an existing person, or does retraction always mint a new one?

The preview copy differs materially between the two: B2 names the destination person and its identity count, which only makes sense if re-homing is possible.

The outcome is binary, and one branch deletes a screen. If retraction always mints, B2 should be deleted rather than shipped as a state that cannot occur — so this is worth resolving before the build starts rather than during it.
